You've seen this before

The Canal

Two ships meet in a narrow canal, too tight for both to pass. Each waits for the other to reverse first. Neither does — so both sit there, engines idling, going nowhere.

🚿
The Bathroom

Two roommates share one bathroom and one hair dryer. One's in the shower, waiting for the dryer to free up. The other's drying their hair, waiting for the shower to free up. Both reasonable. Both late.

🚪
The Doorway

Two people meet in a narrow doorway. Each steps aside to let the other through — and steps into the exact spot the other just left. They do this three or four times before anyone actually gets past.

Notice the shape: each one is waiting on exactly one other thing to move first. Nobody's wrong. Nothing's broken. Everyone's just stuck.

How does something freeze completely when every part of it is just one small step from moving? On the next stop, you'll watch the same shape play out — at a rail junction, with two trains and no dispatcher.

The Yard

How this works

Rules of the yard

  • You control both trains, one move at a time.
  • A train must claim a section before entering it.
  • A claimed section can't be taken back until the train fully clears it.

Your challenge

  • Get both trains through — without a standoff.
  • The order you move them in is the whole game.
  • One bad sequence, and both trains freeze for good.
Simulation

Run the junction

Advance each train in whatever order you like. Watch what happens when trains need the same ground at the same time.

When this is on, a train must hold the system token before entering the junction at all — so only one train can ever be holding shared track, and a circular wait can't form. Watch the 🔑 badge and the amber "queued" trains below.

🔑Right-of-way: open

Deadlock detected

Recover by rolling one train back to the start — this snaps the circular wait, a real technique called deadlock recovery by preemption.

How to actually solve it

  • Break the circular wait: agree on one global order for requesting resources so a cycle can never form.
  • Break hold-and-wait: require a train to grab everything it needs at once, or nothing at all.
  • Allow preemption: let a dispatcher forcibly reclaim a held section from a stalled train.
  • Limit concurrency: flip on Prevention Mode above — only one train may be inside the junction system at a time, so nobody can be left holding one piece while wanting another.
