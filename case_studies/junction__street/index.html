A case study

Junction Street

Mile 1 · A Familiar Problem

You've seen this before

Two ships meet in a narrow canal, too tight for both to pass. Each waits for the other to reverse first. Neither does — both just sit there.

Two roommates share one bathroom and one hair dryer. Each is waiting on the other to finish. Both reasonable. Both stuck.

Notice the shape: each one is waiting on exactly one other thing to move first. Nobody's wrong — everyone's just stuck.

Next stop, you'll watch this exact shape play out at a street crossing.

Mile 4 · Debrief

What just happened?

Before you keep going, think back on the junction.

1. Why didn't any car move at first, even though each driver only needed one spot to open up?
2. What was actually needed to get everyone moving again?

Four things had to be true at once for the junction to freeze completely:

1
Only one car fits at a time. The crossing couldn't be shared — a spot belonged to whoever was in it.
2
Everyone held their ground while waiting. No driver gave up their spot just because they were stuck.
3
Nobody would go out of turn. Everyone followed the rule correctly, so no one broke the pattern on their own.
4
The waiting formed a closed loop. Aarav waited on Rhea, who waited on Meera, who waited on Dev, who waited on Aarav — right back to the start.

Take away any one of those four and the freeze breaks. That's exactly what happened when you waved one driver through, out of turn.

You'll spot this same shape in surprising places — two cooks each holding the tool the other one needs, a group chat where everyone's waiting for someone else to reply first, even two computer programs each holding a file the other one is waiting on.
