🧩 Just Think About It

In many real-life situations, people need to arrange a collection of items according to a particular order. For example, organizing products by price, arranging students by height, ordering files by name, or arranging scores from highest to lowest. When the items are not in the desired order, they need to be rearranged through a series of changes.

This raises an important question: How can we systematically rearrange the items into the desired order while making the necessary changes as efficiently as possible? Solving this problem requires a systematic approach for deciding which items to compare, when to change their positions, and when the desired order has been achieved.

Let's see exactly what that looks like 👇

📚 Chapter 4 · The Photographer's Method

So... What Were We Actually Doing?

Everything you just did in the game has a name. Here's the algorithm behind it.

🫧 It's called Bubble Sort

It's called Bubble Sort because, pass after pass, the largest remaining value keeps "bubbling up" toward the end of the line, one comparison and one possible swap at a time, just like a bubble rising to the surface of water.

The 4 big ideas

🔍 Compare neighbours ▾

Only two students standing right next to each other are ever compared, never elements that are far apart in the line.

🔄 Swap only when needed ▾

If the left one is taller than the right one, they swap places. If they're already in order, nothing happens and we move on.

🫧 One pass, one bubble ▾

By the time a full pass finishes, the largest remaining value has "bubbled" all the way to its correct spot at the end.

✅ Stop when nothing changes ▾

If an entire pass goes by with zero swaps, every element must already be in order, so the algorithm can stop early.
